Open Source Conference Luxembourg

7th of October 2026

Belval, Luxembourg

Leading by example in Digital Sovereignty

While some considered Digital Sovereignty an impossible endeavour others decided to lead by example and invest in Open Source software and skills to show that it is indeed possible.

International cooperation between OSPOs (Open Source Program Offices), members of the Digital Commons EDIC, and many other organisations, associations, and individuals are delivering the solutions that are enabling radical change in how we consume and contribute to digital tools.

This year’s conference will provide participants the opportunity to exchange ideas and experiences on current and planned projects that implement and secure Open Source software for many use cases.

Main topics

Public sector

The public sector has always been one of the major market drivers for the acquisition and implementation of many technologies and standards.

More recently various public sector organisations and institutions have become not only users of Open Source software but also active participants in the development and funding of many projects.

Let’s hear from some of the involved parties about their journey from being passive users of technologies to being active contributors to small and large projects and how their Open Source Programme Office (OSPO) helped with the transition.

Artificial Intelligence

Despite the hype and the recreational uses of new tools that make intensive use of power hungry GPUs there are many areas where Large Language Models (LLMs) and Algorithmic Inference (aka Artificial Intelligence or AI) can bring real benefit to organisations and society.

To make these systems effective they need to ingest large amount of data which often include personal and sensitive information making offerings from hyperscalers unsuitable for many uses.

Many Open Source projects aim to democratise AI allowing small and large organisation to benefit from these new tools while protecting the data being used and reducing the environmental impact of these technologies.

Cyber Security

In a world where practically every device is or has a built in computer security should be the main concern for everyone.

Open Source software helps in reducing the attack surface but it still requires experts that hunts for potential vulnerabilities and coordinate eventual responses.

Luxembourg is renowned for its investments in cyber security and for being a major producer of Open Source tools that help in securing our digital lives.

Cyber security experts will provide you with useful insights on some of the Open Source software that have become essential tools in the fight against cyber threats.

EdTech and academia

There are many projects and communities specialised in the technologies that help the educational sector in provide the Open Source tools that students can use and modify to become the next generation of creators instead of becoming mere consumers of technologies.

The education sector, through universities TTOs (Technology Transfer Offices) and partnership, are also great users and producers of Open Source software which often represents a very large portion of the software of their deliverables.

In the dedicated track local and international universities and EdTech organisations will present how Open Source software is reshaping the education sector.

Decentralised technologies

Public institutions are increasingly aware of the risks of having communications, collaboration and code hosted on commercial platforms owned elsewhere, under terms they do not set and cannot easily leave. The need for decentralised alternatives is no longer a theoretical concern for administrations.

Open protocols mean they can easily switch providers, host services themselves, or share infrastructure with peer organisations, so no single vendor or outage takes down a public service.

In this dedicated track, public sector organisations, developers and hosting experts will present how federated and self-hosted technologies are being deployed in practice, what they deliver in terms of digital sovereignty, and which challenges remain unsolved.

Co-create the Cyber Commons

Join Luxembourg Cybersecurity Factory (LCSF) to explore how trusted open tools, open data and open AI can strengthen Europe’s cyber resilience. Through expert panels and hands-on co-creation sessions, participants will shape practical solutions, from the Cybersecurity Data Space to a Humanitarian Cyber Commons.

Bring your expertise, challenge existing ideas and help turn shared needs into openly accessible, reusable resources for a stronger cybersecurity ecosystem.

Co-creation session will be including Cyber Commons in practice — feedback on the Cybersecurity Factory & Data Space and A Humanitarian Cyber Commons for cybersecurity and AI.
