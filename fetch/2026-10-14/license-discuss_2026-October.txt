Subject: Pre-submission discussion: Zero-Clause BSD with Patent Grant
(0BSD-P)

Hello,

I am considering submitting the Zero-Clause BSD with Patent Grant (0BSD-P)
to OSI for approval. Before doing so, I would like to get feedback from the
community.

I am not a lawyer, and I am not trying to draft a novel license from
scratch.
What I am proposing is a strict combination of two already-approved
licenses:
the Zero-Clause BSD License (0BSD) and the patent grant from the
BSD-2-Clause
Plus Patent License (BSD-2-Clause-Patent).

The gap I see is this: no OSI-approved license currently combines zero
attribution requirements with an express patent grant.

Today, a developer who wants to release code without any attribution
obligation has two OSI-approved options: 0BSD and MIT-0. Neither of them
contains a patent grant. A developer who wants patent protection has options
like BSD-2-Clause-Patent and Apache-2.0, but both require attribution, and
Apache-2.0 additionally includes a patent termination clause that makes it
incompatible with GPLv2.

This means that for small utilities, scripts, generated code, or similar
artifacts where attribution is impractical or undesirable, there is no
OSI-approved way to also grant patent protection. The 0BSD-P is meant to
fill
exactly that gap.

The license text is a direct combination of two approved texts. The
permissive
paragraph is taken verbatim from 0BSD. The patent grant paragraph is taken
from BSD-2-Clause-Patent, with one adaptation: references to "contributor"
and
"Contribution" have been removed, because 0BSD has no conditions that would
create a contributor in the legal sense. The patent grant remains
irrevocable
and does not include a termination clause, which preserves the GPLv2
compatibility that BSD-2-Clause-Patent is known for.

I have attached the full text. I would appreciate any feedback, especially
on:

1. Whether this gap is real and worth filling.
2. Whether the combination is correct.
3. Whether there are any concerns about OSD compliance.

Thank you for your time.
