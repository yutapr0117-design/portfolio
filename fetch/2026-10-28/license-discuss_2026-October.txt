Hello,

I am considering submitting the Zero-Clause BSD with Patent Grant (0BSD-P)
to OSI for approval. Before doing so, I would like to get feedback from the
community.

I am not a lawyer, and I am not trying to draft a novel license from
scratch.
What I am proposing is a strict combination of two already-approved
licenses:
the Zero-Clause BSD License (0BSD) and the patent grant from the
BSD-2-Clause
Plus Patent License (BSD-2-Clause-Patent).

The gap I see is this: no OSI-approved license currently combines zero
attribution requirements with an express patent grant.

Today, a developer who wants to release code without any attribution
obligation has two OSI-approved options: 0BSD and MIT-0. Neither of them
contains a patent grant. A developer who wants patent protection has options
like BSD-2-Clause-Patent and Apache-2.0, but both require attribution, and
Apache-2.0 additionally includes a patent termination clause that makes it
incompatible with GPLv2.

This means that for small utilities, scripts, generated code, or similar
artifacts where attribution is impractical or undesirable, there is no
OSI-approved way to also grant patent protection. The 0BSD-P is meant to
fill
exactly that gap.

The license text is a direct combination of two approved texts. The
permissive
paragraph is taken verbatim from 0BSD. The patent grant paragraph is taken
from BSD-2-Clause-Patent, with one adaptation: references to "contributor"
and
"Contribution" have been removed, because 0BSD has no conditions that would
create a contributor in the legal sense. The patent grant remains
irrevocable
and does not include a termination clause, which preserves the GPLv2
compatibility that BSD-2-Clause-Patent is known for.

I have attached the full text. I would appreciate any feedback, especially
on:

1. Whether this gap is real and worth filling.
2. Whether the combination is correct.
3. Whether there are any concerns about OSD compliance.

Thank you for your time.

Hello,

I would like to get feedback on a new license before any formal submission.
This is about the Model Chain Attribution License (MCAL), which is not a
modification of any existing license but a new license designed for a
specific, measurable problem in the AI supply chain.

The problem is what a 2026 study calls "permissive washing." The study
audited 294,012 dataset -> model -> application supply chains and found
that 96.9% of datasets and 96.4% of models lack the required license text,
even when labeled MIT or Apache-2.0. More importantly, attribution
collapses as artifacts move downstream: only 25.0% of models preserve
compliant dataset notices, and only 5.4% of applications preserve compliant
model notices.

This means that even when upstream components have permissive licenses, the
legal basis for using them is often lost by the time they reach an
application. Companies using these models may be operating outside the
scope of the original license without knowing it.

The MCAL proposes a different mechanism to address this. Instead of
requiring a license file or a copyright notice in every artifact, it
requires a structured, machine-readable Attribution Record. This is a
single, extensible JSON, YAML, or XML file that lists the artifact's
upstream dependencies, their licenses, and their relationship types (e.g.,
"trained-on", "derived-from"). The record propagates automatically through
the chain: from dataset to model to application.

The core question is: is this a real problem that a license can address?
And if so, is a machine-readable attribution record the right mechanism?

I have attached the full text for context. I would appreciate any feedback,
especially on:
1. Whether this problem is worth solving at the license level.
2. Whether the proposed mechanism is feasible and clear.
3. Whether there are any OSD concerns I should be aware of.

Thank you for your time.

--
Mikhail Chernov

I am a member of OpenReciprocal, writing for OpenReciprocal. I would like comments on a
draft license. This is not a license-review submission, and I am not
asking for an SPDX identifier.

In August I posted an AGPLv3 text with a noncommercial exception, and
then retracted it. Bradley Kuhn and McCoy Smith explained why that
construction does not work: the exception is a further restriction, and
OSI does not approve add-ons to an existing license. This draft is a
different document. It does not modify AGPLv3, it is not an exception
to any existing license, and a noncommercial or field-of-use limit is
not allowed. I am not asking anyone to reopen that thread.

The draft is the Open Source Evolution License, version 0.2:

https://github.com/OpenReciprocal/open-source-evolution-license/blob/af546bf/LICENSES/OSEL-0.2-draft.txt

Please use that commit. I will not edit that file while it is under
discussion.

The idea is one sentence. Any recipient may ship a modified copy under
a stricter OSI-approved license. Those stricter terms stick to that
copy and to copies made from it. No other copy is rewritten.

The grant at the start is permissive. Taking the step is optional, and
only the person who actually ships a modified copy can take it, and
only for the copy they ship. The target must be an unmodified
OSI-approved license. It must keep the notices this draft already
requires, add at least one duty from a fixed menu, and add no duty from
outside that menu. The menu is: further attribution, source and
share-alike, patents, names, and change marks. A step that fails the
test is void. Another copy that merely carries the same version label,
and was not made from the shipment, stays under this draft.

No license is pre-approved as a target. I am not asking the list to
accept GPL, AGPL, Apache, MPL, or EUPL. The person who takes the step
has to show that the text they chose meets the test.

Three questions:

1. Is this a gap, or is the right answer dual licensing, or starting
   the project on MPL or EUPL?

2. Section 1.15 says material taken from a sticky copy is sticky to
   the extent of that material. Section 5.5 says stickiness follows
   the copy, not the version label and not the project. Is that
   boundary usable?

3. Does "unmodified OSI-approved license, plus this menu" avoid a
   catalog of license names, or is the menu a catalog with the names
   left off?

The text has not been reviewed by counsel. I am not a lawyer. No
software project outside this repository uses the draft. Version 0.1
was published on 2026-09-26. Version 0.2 does not amend it. The text
under discussion is 0.2.
