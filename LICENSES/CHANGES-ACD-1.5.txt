ACD-1.5 -- CHANGES FROM ACD-1.4 (DRAFT). Not part of the Dedication.

  The reasons for each change are in LICENSES/ACD-1.5-CHANGELIST.md (in
  Japanese).

  Changes in this draft, relative to ACD-1.4:

    * Restatement of the whole text: what ACD-1.4 says in several places is
      said once; the preamble and the sentences that explain other sentences
      are left out, and the clauses are renumbered
      (LICENSES/CLAUSE-MAP-1.4-1.5.tsv). It covers Sections 1.1 to 1.12, 2.1
      to 2.7, 3.1 to 3.2, 4.1 to 4.4, 5.1 to 5.6, 6.1 to 6.5, 7.1 to 7.4, 8.1
      to 8.2, 9.1 to 9.3, and 10.1 to 10.7. (-2400 words.)
    * ACD-1.4: 5879 words, 80 clauses. This draft: 3479 words, 52 clauses.

  What we know to differ in effect (this list is kept by hand, and readers have
  found omissions in it; the two texts are the authority):

    1. A Dedicator is each person who applies the Dedication, whether or not a
       right subsists; as to each Dedicator, the Work is the material to which
       that Dedicator applied it, and the text of the Dedication is not part of
       it (Sections 1.2 and 1.3; errata E93).
    2. Moral Rights include those of a performer (Section 1.5; errata E92).
    3. A person bound as a successor or transferee is not a Dedicator as to what
       that person holds otherwise than from the Dedicator (Section 1.3), and
       utility models are named (Section 1.12).
    4. A Result may result from the Work indirectly. A copy of the Work in any
       form, object code compiled from it included, is not itself a Result; a
       patent claim on such a copy is reached, if at all, under Section 2.3(a),
       or, as to the automated processing that makes it, under Section 2.3(b)
       (Section 1.8).
    5. The patent licence reaches subject matter in the Work, which is the
       material to which the Dedicator applied the Dedication, including, where
       the Work is a Contribution, by its combination with the material it was
       submitted for inclusion in, and an infringement on any theory (Section
       2.3(a); errata E89).
    6. As to a Result, the patent licence reaches a feature that the Work
       teaches or embodies, that is extracted from its content, or that is
       learned from its content, alone or together with other material; ACD-1.4
       reaches a feature that results, in whole or in part, from that content,
       and leaves every feature added to the Work to its Section 8.1 (Section
       2.3(b)). Narrower: a feature the Work played only a part in producing.
       Wider: a feature added to the Work that was learned from the Work
       together with other material, or that combines what the Work teaches with
       what another text teaches. That the Work was processed, or was the
       starting point, does not by itself show that a feature was learned or
       extracted from it. The same test governs Computational Use. Use of the
       Work only to evaluate, align, select, or steer, so that a feature results
       from it only in part, may be read as outside it for that reason.
    7. Everyone who receives the Work, anything derived from it, or a Result
       holds the licences directly from the Dedicator (Section 2.4).
    8. The covenants as to Moral Rights and as to other claims reach what the
       Dedicator holds (Section 2.5(c)), and a claim that the Dedicator holds or
       controls, or that another holds through the Dedicator (Section 2.5(d)).
       The covenant as to other claims extends to authorising or assisting
       another person; the second sentence of ACD-1.4's Section 5.1 speaks of
       the Dedicator's own assertion. The covenants speak of exercising a right
       as well as of asserting it (Section 2.5); ACD-1.4 does so only as to
       Moral Rights. Wider: the Dedicator gives up more.
    9. The covenant as to other claims is about a claim that treats a use as
       wrongful by reason of its being a use of the Work or of a Result, or of
       what the Work contains (Section 2.5(d); ACD-1.1-SELF-AUDIT.md, sections
       3zf and 3zh). That is narrower than both the general words of ACD-1.4's
       Section 5.1 and its Section 5.2(d), which carried no such limit. Outside
       it are a claim for an attack made with the Work, and a claim by the
       Dedicator under a right of privacy, publicity, or personality that rests
       on neither of those reasons, such as one for an image, or an imitation of
       the voice, of the Dedicator that a person makes with the Work from
       something other than the Work. Read as written, ACD-1.4 stopped each of
       those claims. A claim that rests on the manner or effect of a use, such
       as one for unfair competition or for the load placed on a server, may be
       read as outside it, and so may a claim by the Dedicator in respect of the
       Dedicator's own likeness or voice contained in the Work, if it rests on
       the manner or effect of the use; ACD-1.4's Section 5.1 stopped the first
       and its Section 5.2(d) the second. Section 9.1 is against that reading.
   10. Nothing is imposed in respect of a Result, as well as of the Work, and
       the covenant on collateral obligations, the sentence on requests, and the
       rule of construction speak of a Result too; and a person bound as a
       transferee is named beside the Dedicator where the absence of conditions,
       and the freedom to choose terms, do not reach (Sections 4.1, 4.3, 4.4,
       5.1, and 9.1; ACD-1.1-SELF-AUDIT.md, section 3ze). Section 4.3 leaves in
       place whatever Section 5 forbids a person to impose; ACD-1.4's Section
       4.5 speaks of material as to which that person is a Dedicator. Narrower
       for a person who passes the Work on and is bound as a transferee without
       being a Dedicator.
   11. The covenant on technological measures speaks of, and authorises
       circumvention for, a copy that has been made available (Section 5.2).
       Narrower: ACD-1.4 speaks of the Work, and of a copy of it, without that
       limit. Wider: the authorisation is for the purpose of a use; ACD-1.4
       speaks of a circumvention effected in a use.
   12. The covenant not to assert a narrower reading of a permission (ACD-1.4's
       Section 5.2(c)) is left out; Section 9.1 governs the reading.
   13. The exceptions from the covenants are stated covenant by covenant
       (Sections 5.4 to 5.6). Rights of privacy, publicity, or personality are
       taken out of the exception for a name (Section 5.4(a)). A term about a
       Contribution, asked of the person submitting it, is outside the covenants
       in Sections 2.5(d) and 5.1 unless it restricts, or imposes an obligation
       in respect of, use of the Work or of any Result (Section 5.4(d)). ACD-1.4
       excepts a service only where no copy of the Work is provided; here the
       exception covers a service that provides a copy, other than as to that
       copy (Section 5.5(b)). The exceptions for patent infringement outside the
       patent licence and for a false statement apply to the covenant in Section
       5.1 as well; those in ACD-1.4's Section 5.2(b) are for rights in
       trademarks and names and for an agreed payment. Narrower for a recipient
       on whom a term of that kind is imposed.
   14. The omission of attribution alone is not a false statement (Section 6.3).
   15. The statement in ACD-1.4's Section 11.4 that the Dedication reaches
       nothing else is gone (ACD-1.1-SELF-AUDIT.md, section 3zd). In its place
       Section 6.1 says that the Dedication gives nothing by implication beyond
       what its words give.
   16. Neither passing on or recording another person's notice with the material
       it applies to, nor mentioning the Dedication, applies it to anything
       unless done to apply it (Section 10.2; errata E90).
   17. Each person who made and submits a Contribution, or on whose authority it
       is submitted, applies the Dedication to it, unless that person or the
       person submitting it states otherwise (Section 10.4).
   18. ACD-1.4's preamble, its Section 8.3, which says that no patent claim is
       reserved, the sentences of purpose in its Sections 6.5 and 12.3, the
       sentence of its Section 9.2 that the Dedication adds nothing where no
       right subsists, the sentence of its Section 9.4 that Section 9 is not a
       disclaimer of the fact of origin, and the sentence of its Section 16.3
       that it is not specific to any project, person, or field, are left out.
   19. ACD-1.4's Section 6.4 says that no enforceable claim arises in respect of
       a model or an output. Here that is a covenant (Section 2.5(a)) and an
       absence of conditions (Section 4.1).
   20. A Contribution is any material, not only a work of authorship, and not
       material submitted to a repository or forum that puts such submissions
       under other terms instead (Section 1.11; ACD-1.1-SELF-AUDIT.md, section
       3zg). A person who, without stating otherwise, submits to a project that
       offers the Work under this Dedication but puts submissions under other
       terms, gives recipients nothing under this Dedication, patents included;
       ACD-1.4 reached that person.
   21. The warranties and the kinds of loss are named in shorter lists (Sections
       8.1 and 8.2). Section 8.2 also names the use of any Result. ACD-1.4's
       Section 14.2 says that a liability that cannot be excluded is limited to
       the greatest extent the law permits; here Section 8.2 does not limit a
       liability that the law does not permit to be limited. The Dedicator loses
       that sentence.
   22. The suggested notice says that the work is provided as is, and points to
       Section 2.5(d) (Section 10.1).
   23. A person to whom a Covered Right or a Patent Claim is later exclusively
       licensed is named beside a transferee, and a person entitled to exercise
       or enforce the Dedicator's Moral Rights is bound as to Sections 2.5(c)
       and 2.6, however the entitlement arose (Section 7.2). Section 7.2(b)
       speaks of a later transfer, where ACD-1.4's Section 2.8 speaks of a
       transfer, and its sentence that a transferee takes subject to the
       Dedication is not repeated. Read as the same in effect.
   24. The covenant against collateral obligations reaches a Result only by
       reason of its being a Result; a term asked of a contributor may not
       impose an obligation in respect of use of the Work or of any Result;
       those entitled to the Dedicator's Moral Rights are bound only as to
       Sections 2.5(c) and 2.6; and the marking that excludes material from
       being a Contribution is made when the material is submitted (Sections
       1.11, 5.1, 5.4(d), and 7.2).
   25. Readings that a reader raised and that were accepted, not repaired (the
       reasons are in LICENSES/ACD-1.5-CLARITY-TRIAL.md, section 13, in
       Japanese): (a) the terms a Dedicator applies to its own material may
       include measures (Section 5.6; ACD-1.4 says the same of those terms); (b)
       a person to whom a patent claim is later exclusively licensed, or who
       buys one, is bound by Section 5.1 as well (Section 7.2; ACD-1.4's
       Sections 2.8 and 5.3 bind a transferee in respect of the Work); (c) a
       Contribution that contains another person's ACD material makes the person
       who submits it a Dedicator as to it, for the rights that person holds
       (Sections 1.6, 1.7, and 10.4), although Section 10.2 says that passing on
       a notice applies nothing; (d) a person who takes an assignment and
       applies the Dedication itself may be read as having to mark material that
       the Dedication already covers (Section 10.4); and (e) Section 6.1
       excludes implication and Section 9.1 takes the widest reading (they are
       read together; ACD-1.4 pairs the statement of reach in its Section 11.4
       with its Section 15.1).
   26. The right to remuneration that is surrendered is one "under the Covered
       Rights" (Section 2.1); ACD-1.4's Section 3.2 speaks of one arising from
       any use of the Work.
   27. The sentence on silence, delay, and non-enforcement speaks of any copy of
       the Work that has been made available (Section 7.1); ACD-1.4's Section
       2.2 puts no such limit on that sentence.
   28. Those who may rely on the covenants as intended beneficiaries include
       anyone who receives anything derived from the Work, or a Result, from
       anyone, and the covenants include the one as to Moral Rights (Section
       7.4); ACD-1.4's Section 5.3 speaks of anyone who receives the Work from
       You, directly or indirectly, and does not name its Section 12.2. Wider
       for such a person.
   29. The Dedicator makes no representation that a right subsists in a part of
       the Work generated by an automated system (Section 3.2); ACD-1.4's
       Section 9.2 says so of any part. Section 8.1 disclaims a warranty for the
       rest. ACD-1.4's Section 1.8 defines machine-generated material as what is
       produced in whole or in substantial part by such a system; that
       definition is gone. Read as the same in effect; the Dedicator loses by it
       if Section 8.1 is read more narrowly.
   30. The permission to copy this text is shorter, and a new version that the
       Steward publishes may be distributed by anyone; ACD-1.4's Section 16.4
       excepts the Steward, and says that it applies to a new version too
       (Section 10.5). Wider for a person who distributes a new version.
   31. A notice is sufficient where a Dedicator places it; ACD-1.4's Section
       16.2 speaks of a notice that accompanies material to which a Dedicator
       has applied the Dedication, without saying who places it (Section 10.2).
       Narrower for a recipient where someone other than a Dedicator placed the
       notice.
