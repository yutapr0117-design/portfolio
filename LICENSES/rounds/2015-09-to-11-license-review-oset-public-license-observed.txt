On 09/09/2015 03:31 PM, Lawrence Rosen wrote:
> Nigel, please don't put me in the false position of claiming that GPLv3
> is good for everything. You know it has never been my favorite license.
> :-)  But in this case, considering the essential element of public trust
> in public election software, I want the (psychologically) strongest
> license in our arsenal. I DO want the public to be comforted by
> attorneys at the ready to defend software freedom for this GPL
> application. That will be reassuring around the world.

*shrug* I agree with you, but that is not our job.

Our job is to decide:

a) does the OSET license meet the letter and spirit of the OSD?

b) does it contain unique and valid provisions, such that no existing
license is sufficient?

c) is it legally sound?

d) is it going to be used by a significant and sustained project?

Any discussion about which license OSET should adopt and whether they
really need their provisions or not should take place in other forums.
 You can do what you want, but I am not going to try to tell someone
else what license they should pick for their project, and in my opinion
such posts have no place in license-review.

--Josh Berkus

David,


> I would contend that the review would fail on this criteria:
> 
> b) does it contain unique and valid provisions, such that no existing
> license is sufficient?
> 
> The existing GPL3 "as is" is sufficient for the stated purpose of

That's a BS argument and you know it.  I could just as well argue that
the BSD license is really "sufficient" for all OSS and we should abolish
all other licenses.  But we're not going to do that, so please stop
trying to disguise an ideological argument as a legal one.  It's not
working, and it's making you look stupid doing it.

It is NOT OUR BUSINESS to tell an OSS project that they should have a
different license.  If you want to argue that OSET should use the GPLv3
instead, then join their project and argue it in their community.

> OSET provided a link to their web site and their stated objectives - it
> appears the real purpose of the OSET license is to provide the criteria
> for inclusion in their own repository.

Given that this is exactly what Apache, Eclipse, and Mozilla do, and all
of those licenses are certified by the OSI, this is actually an argument
*for* the license, not against it.

--Josh Berkus
