To be completely above-board: My long-term goal here -- which I think
others on this list share -- is to make the list a more welcoming place
for newcomers who a) don't have personal history with the organization
or with others on these lists, and b) don't necessarily have the thick
skins that participation here has, um, historically required.

My thesis is that the quality of license analysis will go up, not down,
if we can attract & keep some of those people.  Which is not to say the
quality is low now.  It's just that there is a high S-to-N ratio and
it's hard sometimes to extract the oil from the shale.

(My other goal is improving my metaphors.)

Ah, thanks -- knowing that context helps.

One of the things that's tricky on these lists, I think, is that two
interlocutors may know each other personally, but *that fact* may be
unknown to others on the list (as it was to me, for example)... Which
means that gentle-but-public chiding between old acquaintances can cause
others to think that that kind chiding is appropriate between strangers,
thus unintentionally contributing to a general anything-goes atmosphere.

The solution, I think, is not to talk to everyone as though they're a
stranger, but just to make sure that the context is clear when you're
addressing someone familiarly -- so that observers know that's what's
going on.

(And I realize this is a lot of meta-conversation here, but I think it's
needed to avoid other threads getting as heated as CC0 got.)
