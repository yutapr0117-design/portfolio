# Reviewing ACD — the record, point by point

This page holds two parts of [`REVIEWERS.md`](REVIEWERS.md), moved here on 2026-11-24 so that the
entry point is shorter. No sentence was changed in the move. The weaknesses, the disclosures and the
strongest thing against each part of the case stay on the entry point; the complete adverse record is
[`ACD-1.0.against.md`](ACD-1.0.against.md).

## What the record already says in its favour

**What the record already says in its favour.** These are quoted from the lists, the Committee's
rationale documents, the Board's minutes and OSI's site. None of them binds anyone: *"Approval of a license with the same or similar terms in the
past does not bind the OSI"*, and a list message is its author's own view. Nor are the rows an argument that ACD should pass because an approved licence did the same: Carlo Piana, 2024-09-06, *"Please never use the "but license x does the same" argument. If there was a mistake in approving one license, this does not mean the same mistake must be repeated over and over."* Each row shows how the OSD or a criterion has been read; ACD has to meet them on its own text. Clause numbers in this table are those of ACD-1.4; where 1.0 numbers a clause differently, its number follows in parentheses.

The same FAQ says *"rather than try to waive copyright altogether"*, which is §3, and values attribution, which §10.2 gives up |

The same article calls Apache-2.0's clause *"a form of mutual patent peace"*, Berkus goes on *"We've also learned the value of non-proliferation"*, and neither is a decision |

Richard Fontana said UPL's breadth *"goes way beyond to the opposite extreme"*, and before approval its grant was narrowed to the unmodified Software and the listed works. Henrik Ingo named *"the surprise element"* for contributors (#313). In the Blue Oak review, approved in 2024, Carlo Piana answered the same worry: *"those holding a patent portfolio and not wishing to dilute it in a non-copyleft license understand the consequences of using such license"* |
| Surrender plus licence | An article on OSI's site, 2017: *"A solution would be"* to *"disclaim ownership for the jurisdictions where that is possible, and then grant a copyright license that has the same practical effect"*; CC0 stalled *"during a complex discussion about patent rights"* | §3 and §4 are those two parts, §4 is not limited to those jurisdictions (§4.4), and §8 grants patents. The article is one author's, not a decision, and ends by recommending MIT |
| Liability, in the public domain | The same FAQ: *"we don't know how or whether the author would be protected from liability for software released into the public domain"* | §13 and §14 |
| Liability, in a dedication | McCoy Smith, 2016-08-16, of CC0: *"I believe that to be an effective waiver of liability, despite the fact that there is not copyright rights being conveyed."* | §13 and §14. Richard Fontana read CC0 differently in 2017: *"CC0 does not have a limitation of liability provision as far as I can tell"*; ACD has both a warranty disclaimer and a limitation. Against that: in 2002 Russell Nelson, then handling licence approval for the OSI, passed on the view that a licence that is not a contract cannot make the recipient give up warranty rights (*"Only a contract can do that."*), and ACD asks for no assent (§2.3). Bruce Perens disputed the authority cited; GPL-2.0 and other approved licences that need no assent also disclaim warranty. How far the disclaimer holds without a contract is a question for each jurisdiction, and no lawyer has looked (`ACD-1.0.against.md` #321) |
| Why CC0 stopped | Board minutes, 2012-03-07: *"Recommend disapprove, due to clause 4a"*. A straw poll that month went 13 to 8 against approval; all 13 gave the patent exclusion among their reasons and none gave the structure of waiver plus licence (our count, `rounds/2012-03-license-review-cc0-straw-poll-observed-1of2.txt`). One of the 13, Chris DiBona: *"I don't think that any new licenses should be approved unless they include patent clauses"*. Richard Fontana, who raised the point, 2019-05: *"I was not troubled by the idea of approval of CC0 as such."* In 2019-08 he also called the outcome a rejection, in quotation marks: *"for the most part the OSI has not formally rejected licenses at all, yet often the non-approval disposition of a license submission is (at least arguably) equivalent to a rejection"*, and *"the "rejection" (i.e., withdrawal after negative license-review reaction) of CC0"*. It was not formally rejected; it did not get through. Fedora dropped CC0 for code in 2022 and still allows the Unlicense: *"licenses that preclude any form of patent licensing or patent forbearance cannot be considered FOSS"* (Fontana, as LWN quotes him). During the review Lawrence Rosen, 2012-02: *"There is nothing we have identified about the CC0 license that comes close to violating any of the provisions of the OSD."* | §8 replaces the exclusion with a grant. No review has tested that form. Russ Nelson objected to it (`ACD-1.0.objection-map.md`), CC0's academic users wanted the exclusion (*"the patent exception is important to them"*), and the replacement Fontana recommended, MIT-0, says nothing of patents (#290) |
| The word "dedication" | Karl Fogel, 2012-01-02: *"The fact that one is called a "license" and the other is called a "dedication" does not lessen their essential similarity"*, and of such promises, *"Those sorts of promises are what the OSI evaluates and sometimes approves."* | The name. He adds: *"We could decide, for other reasons, not to evaluate any PD dedications."* |

Richard Fontana, 2015-11-12: *"the OSI obtained outside legal counsel concerning the legal and policy issues raised by 'ultra-permissive' licenses"* | §10.1, including no notice. The same minutes ask *"is it really going to be used?"*. The advice is not public, so we cannot say it was favourable or that it reaches ACD |
| A dedication with a licence beside it | Carlo Piana, 2024-12-18: *"I have nothing against using a PD dedication and a license by way of backstop"*, *"I actually advise to use both in the admittedly rare case I am asked."* McCoy Smith, same day: having both is *"not necessarily fatal"* if the relation is stated clearly | §3 and §4, and §4.4 states the relation |
| A dedication with a patent promise | Karl Fogel, 2014-05-04: *"maybe we wouldn't certify PD itself for software works, but would certify PD *when accompanied by* a particular patent non-assertion text"*, adding *"it's more complex than that"*. Bruce Perens, 2012-02-26, on what a public-domain text would need: *"a public domain declaration coupled with a covenant not-to-sue that extends to the patent claims of the dedicator that are necessary to utilize the work as it was dedicated. And a warranty disclaimer to protect the donor."* Gervase Markham, 2017-02-28: *"if the scheme had a patent grant accompanying the CC-0 license, that might solve both of these issues in one go and lead to something very, very good."* McCoy Smith, 2016-08-16: *"[although CC0 with a plug-in patent grant might work....]"* Richard Fontana, writing from opensource.org, 2016-12-13: *"If the US government standardizes on some particular explicit patent language to use with CC0 I would welcome OSI review of that."* | §3 with §8.6, and §13. Perens began by doubting any *"benefit"* over BSD and ended *"It ends up not being shorter nor simpler."* His patent scope stops at the work, and §8.4 goes further (#300, #305). Smith added in 2017 that his plug-in *"isn't OSI approved (nor would it likely ever be as it's not an independent license)"*; ACD is one text |

| The need | Christopher Sean Morrison, 2017-10-26: *"so many federal agencies moving into open source, commensurately searching the legal landscape for something close to public domain yet still addressing indemnification and contributor patent grants"*. A talk hosted on OSI's site, 2025, on existing licences and training data: *"Unclear how attribution requirements transfer through the AI training lifecycle"*. Clark C. Evans, in the review of CC0, 2012-02-25: *"an instrument that is succinct, covers patents as well as copyrights, is both a dedication and fallback doesn't exist."* | §3, §2.6 and §8, and §10.2 asks for no attribution. These are stated needs, not adoptions. Morrison goes on: *"All leading to proliferation"*, the talk says *"A one-size-fits-all approach may not work"*, and ACD is not succinct. Mark Radcliffe, as OSI's outside General Counsel, 2016: *"the drafting of a new "open source" license requires strong reasons."* (#314) |
| Burdens on the licensee | Pamela Chestek, 2025-03-02, on ModelGo: *"Imposing too many burdens and obligations on the licensee means that benefits of the open source ecosystem won't be realized."* The same day, in her personal capacity: licences impose requirements *"only to the extent they assure that the licensor receives the benefit that is their motive for putting software under an open source license and to protect the licensor from harm"*. Carlo Piana, 2025-03-03: *"The output of a program (and of an AI system/model alike) should not be subject matter controlled by the maker of them."* | §10.1 imposes none, and §6.4 leaves outputs free |
| One steward | Bradley Kuhn, 2018: *"OSI really does need to trust the license steward when an upgrade clause is in play"*. The Board's minute approving UPL names *"Oracle intends to release software soon"* (#312) |

| AI and ML | The Board, 2025-07-18: *"When licenses aimed at AI/ML projects are approved"*, and such a licence is reviewed against the OSD and the standard criteria. OSI's page of questions on the Open Source AI Definition, on parameters: *"They may be free by nature, or a license or other legal instrument may be required"*, and *"we require an explicit assertion"* that they are free | ACD is reviewed on those terms, and it gives that assertion without assuming a right subsists (§9.2, §16.3). OSAID is not the approval standard. ## Reasons ACD-1.4 no longer gives in its own words

Several sentences of ACD-1.4 say less than 1.3 did, because a reason written into a clause
can be false in some country, and a reader can then use the false reason to narrow the rule. The
rules stayed; the reasons moved here. The full records are in the change list, in Japanese.

| Clause | What ACD-1.4 keeps | The reason, and why it left the text |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| §11.3 | A false statement that the Dedicator wrote or endorses something is outside the Dedication's reach | Earlier versions said this was never the Dedicator's to permit. Under some laws the person named can consent (UK, CDPA s.84 and s.87(1)), so that reason was false there. The limit needs no reason: a permission does not make a false statement true, which is also what answers a recipient who falsely claims to have written the Work |
| §8.4 | No threshold of how much of the Work was used; the test is causation, not quantity | A recipient usually cannot measure how much of a work shaped a model. The reason was removed because someone fine-tuning on their own data sometimes can, and a reason that fails for them invites a threshold for them |
| §6.5 | Machine learning and text and data mining are permitted expressly | Earlier versions said silence had proved ambiguous in practice; we could not source that claim. ACD-1.4 says silence leaves it to be inferred whether this is permitted, which is true everywhere, including where unnamed uses are read narrowly (Germany, UrhG §31(5)). Since trial T108 it also says that *"Section 6 operates on its own footing (Section 2.4)"*, so §6 is a permission in its own right and not only a restatement of §3 to §5 and §8 |
| §10.4 | No permission terminates under the Dedication | Earlier versions said none terminates for any reason. In the United States an author may end a licence after 35 years notwithstanding any agreement (17 U.S.C. §203; `ACD-1.0.jurisdictions.md` §5a), so the text now speaks only of itself |
| §14.1 | Liability for wilful misconduct, gross negligence, death or personal injury is excepted where the law does not permit it to be excluded, and nothing in Section 14 limits it | In Germany an exclusion that reaches these may fall away entirely, and a clause that only limits them may fall with it. EUPL-1.2 §8 excepts wilful misconduct and damage to natural persons outright; ACD-1.4, like Apache-2.0 §8, excepts them only where the law requires it (`ACD-1.0.jurisdictions.md` §3). This row is an addition rather than a removed reason; it is here because the same countries are the reason |
